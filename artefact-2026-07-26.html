What To Actually Watch For Before Your Child's Next Trial

Not fitness. Not tricks. This is what actually gets watched.

Most football trial preparation gets pointed at the wrong things, speed, fitness, a new trick nobody has seen the player try before. What actually gets noticed at a trial has almost nothing to do with any of that.

These are the five things I am actually watching for when I stand at the side of a trial, whether it is for the team up the road or something further up the pathway. Use them to know what to look for in your own child before trial day, not to load anything new onto them at the last minute.

1

Checking the shoulder

Does your child look around before the ball arrives, more than once, not just as a habit they were told to perform. This is the single biggest thing separating players who look calm on the ball from players who don't.

2

Open body shape on the first touch

Do they receive the ball side on, with options in front of them, or do they take a touch that closes everything down before they've decided what to do next.

3

Both feet

Can they actually use their weaker foot under a bit of pressure, or does everything funnel onto the same side. A player who can only go one way becomes easy to predict the moment things speed up.

4

Playing forward when it's on

Are they looking to progress the ball when the chance is there, or always taking the safe option sideways just to stay tidy and avoid a mistake.

5

Confidence in what they already do well

A trial is not the moment to debut something new. The players who stand out are usually the ones playing to their existing strengths with confidence, not the ones trying to invent a new one under pressure.

If your child doesn't do most of these instinctively yet, that's completely normal. These are coachable habits, not fixed traits, and a few focused weeks before a trial is genuinely enough time to build them.
