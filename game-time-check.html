The Game Time Check

What to do when your child is not getting game time. Four steps, the exact questions to ask, and the red flags to watch for.

I've been the coach deciding who goes on. I've also been the dad watching my own son fight for minutes at a JPL team. This is the checklist I'd want in your pocket. Work through it in order. Don't jump straight to step four. Tap any box to tick it off.
!

First, what does fair game time look like?

Under 7 to 11

The FA's goal at these ages is equal playing time for everyone.

JPL

At least 50% of every match a player is selected for.

Under 12 and up

Selection matters more. Your child still deserves an explanation.

1

Ask the coach, the right way

Before you ask

  • Not on the touchline.
  • Not straight after a defeat.
  • Not in the team WhatsApp group.
  • Ask for five minutes away from training instead.

Questions to ask

  • "What does my child need to work on to get more minutes?"
  • "Where do you see them in the squad right now?"
  • "What could they work on outside team training?"
  • "Is there anything we can help with at home?"
  • "When would be a good time to check in again?"
Avoid: "Why isn't my child playing?" It puts the coach on the defensive and you'll learn far less.
2

Act on what you hear

  • Write down what the coach said, in their words.
  • Pick one or two things to work on. Not ten.
  • Have an honest chat with your child. Here's the area, here's maybe why, here's what we can do.
  • Find extra football outside team training. The park, the garden, or one to one sessions.
  • Check back in with the coach after a few weeks.

What the coach told us

"For things to change, we have to change."What I told my own son.
3

Watch for the real red flag

Low minutes on their own aren't the warning sign. Low minutes plus no feedback is. Tick any that are true.

  • My child is hardly getting on, week after week.
  • I've asked the coach and got no clear reason.
  • I'm not told how my child is progressing.
  • My child hasn't been given anything to work on.
  • I have no idea how I could help at home.
  • My child's confidence or enjoyment is dropping.
Ticked most of these after an honest conversation and some real work? Move to step four.
Remember: during training and matches, step away. That's for the coach and your child to work out.
4

Before you move clubs, do your homework

Questions to ask a new club

  • "Who would actually coach my child?"
  • "How do you develop players at this age?"
  • "How do you decide game time?"
  • "How do you keep parents updated on progress?"
  • "Can my child come to a few sessions before we commit?"

Check yourself

  • I've watched my child in a few sessions there.
  • The level is close to where my child is now.
  • I'm confident they'll actually get chances to play.
  • I'm moving for the right environment, not the first team that said yes.
